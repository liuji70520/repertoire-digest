移植物会“记住”：把肾移植排斥重新定义为一个时间闭环

The allograft remembers: Defining the rejection time loop in kidney transplantation

Molecular immunology/2026-11-20/期刊 JournalIF 3.0JCR Q3中科院 3区
做了什么What

综述指出排斥目前被记成“活检发现—治疗—缓解”的离散序列，但随访活检显示缓解常常不彻底：抗体介导排斥治疗后微血管炎症持续、T 细胞介导排斥在功能恢复后仍存在、残留排斥相关 T 细胞克隆型、分子层面排斥活性与移植物细胞转录异常都已被分别证实。作者用“rejection time loop”把这些串成纵向关系，并明确不主张残留损伤已被证明导致下一次排斥；真正的问题是：一次事件后剩余的生物学状态，是否改变后续排斥的表型与预后。连续活检、分子评估与免疫克隆追踪使这个问题变得可检验。

为什么重要Why

对我们有用的不是肾移植本身，而是它把“组库追踪”当成打通时间闭环的关键读数：克隆型不是一张快照，而是能把多次事件连成连续状态的纵向标记。我们做 TCR 克隆分析时，常把克隆型当成某次采样里的扩增程度，这篇提示可以按“残留克隆是否随下一次事件再扩增”来组织多次采样的数据。

This review argues that rejection is recorded as discrete biopsy-defined episodes with treatment and apparent resolution, yet follow-up biopsies show incomplete resolution: persistent microvascular inflammation after treated antibody-mediated rejection, persistent T-cell-mediated rejection despite functional recovery, residual rejection-associated T-cell clonotypes, continued molecular rejection activity and persistent graft-cell transcriptional abnormalities. The authors group these under a 'rejection time loop' describing their longitudinal relationship, explicitly without claiming residual injury is proven to cause the next episode, and pose the testable question of whether the biological state remaining after one episode modifies the phenotype or prognosis of subsequent rejection, now addressable with serial histology, molecular assessment and immune-clonal tracking.

The transferable point is treating clonal tracking as the readout that closes the loop over time: a clonotype is not a single snapshot but a longitudinal marker linking successive events, which reframes multi-sample TCR data around whether residual clones re-expand at the next event.

The allograft remembers: Defining the rejection time loop in kidney transplantation
Molecular immunology · 2026-11-20 · 期刊 Journal · IF 3.0 · JCR Q3 · 中科院 3区
Kidney allograft rejection is commonly recorded as a sequence of biopsy-defined episodes followed by treatment and resolution. Follow-up biopsy studies, however, show that resolution is frequently incomplete. Persistent microvascular inflammation after treated antibody-mediated rejection, persistent T cell-mediated rejection despite apparent functional recovery, residual rejection-associated T-cell clonotypes, continued molecular rejection activity and persistent graft-cell transcriptional abnormalities have each been demonstrated. These observations are currently described separately as persistent rejection, incomplete resolution or residual molecular injury. We use rejection time loop to describe their longitudinal relationship: rejection is treated, but the post-treatment allograft may remain biologically different from its prerejection state when it is subsequently reassessed or develops further alloimmune injury. The term does not imply that residual injury has been proven to cause the next rejection episode. Rather, it identifies temporal continuity that is lost when rejection episodes are analysed independently or previous rejection is reduced to a binary covariate. The relevant research question is whether the biological state remaining after one episode modifies the phenotype or prognosis of subsequent rejection.
