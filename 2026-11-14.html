Molecular biology reports · 2026-11-12 · 期刊 Journal · IF≈3.7
Myocardial infarction (MI) alters the cardiac immune microenvironment through tissue injury, antigen release, and recruitment of innate and adaptive immune cells. Major histocompatibility complex (MHC)-restricted antigen presentation can connect these events to T-cell responses that influence repair or persistent inflammation. This narrative review examines antigen acquisition, initial T-cell priming in cardiac-draining lymph nodes, and subsequent recognition of peptide-MHC complexes within injured myocardium. We integrate these processes with T-cell receptor (TCR) repertoire studies and a spatial framework encompassing the infarct core, border zone, and remote myocardium. Direct experimental evidence for cardiac antigen-specific responses is distinguished from human repertoire associations, findings in other diseases, and proposed mechanisms. In particular, TCR clonal expansion alone does not establish cardiac antigen specificity, and cardiomyocyte antigen display should be separated from professional antigen-presenting cell function. We also consider vascular and lymphatic remodeling, macrophage heterogeneity, and the effects of sex and age. In parallel, tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) have emerged as intratumoral sites of B-cell maturation and tumor-reactive antibody production, with growing associations with immune-checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) response. Sustained MRD-Negative Complete Remission of T-PLL With CNS Involvement Following Treatment With Systemic and Intrathecal Alemtuzumab
EJHaem · 2026-11-11 · 期刊 Journal · IF≈1.51
Background T-cell prolymphocytic leukaemia (T-PLL) with central nervous system (CNS) involvement is uncommon and evidence guiding its treatment is limited. Case summary We describe the successful treatment of T-PLL with leptomeningeal involvement using intrathecal alemtuzumab followed by allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation. Residual disease monitoring of T-cell receptor clonotypes by next-generation sequencing was performed to assess treatment response and guide post-SCT intervention. At 24 months post-SCT, the patient remains in sustained molecular remission. Conclusion This case adds to the limited literature supporting activity of intrathecal alemtuzumab in refractory CNS disease and highlights the utility of molecular monitoring following SCT.
